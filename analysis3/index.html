Structural Analysis

We can try to look at all the words, all at once

When we represent the entire diary as a series of data points, we get a bird's-eye view of the volume and cadence of the text.

But this isn’t very meaningful for us

Without context, the visualization is just a collection of bars. It tells us how much was written, but not the nature of what was written.

Now, what if we assign each sentence a label?

By categorizing sentences based on the author's mode—Social, Event, or Reflection—we begin to see the architecture of the memory.

Then, our zoomed out view becomes quite valuable

The colors reveal a hidden rhythm. We can see seasons of deep introspection followed by bursts of social activity or political reporting.

We can notice patterns and click on sentences to return to the text

The data doesn't replace the diary; it acts as a map. Clicking any segment brings us back to the original intimate prose.

Now, take a moment to explore the tool

Hover over segments to see the category, and click to read the specific sentence. Use the map to navigate the life of the author.
